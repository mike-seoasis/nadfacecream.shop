The skin under the eyes is thin, mobile, and more likely to show dryness, fine lines, puffiness, visible blood vessels, and pigment changes. “Wrinkles” in this area can come from collagen changes, repeated facial movement, dehydration, irritation, and sun exposure. “Dark circles” can come from shadows, hollowness, genetics, pigmentation, allergies, lack of sleep, or vascular tone. Because the causes differ, one face cream cannot be expected to correct every type.

In dermatology, the best-supported cosmetic approaches for the eye area are usually consistent sun protection, moisturization, barrier support, and proven ingredients used at tolerable strengths. Even then, improvement is often modest and gradual. If dark circles appeared suddenly, are one-sided, come with swelling, pain, rash, or eye symptoms, it is better to seek medical advice rather than trying to solve it with skincare.

What the evidence says about topical NAD+ and nicotinamide riboside

NAD+ is a molecule involved in cellular energy metabolism and repair processes. Nicotinamide riboside is a precursor that the body can use in NAD+ pathways. This makes the topic interesting for skincare, but interest is not the same as settled clinical proof.

For topical NAD skincare, the evidence is still early. There is scientific rationale for supporting skin cell function and barrier resilience, but there are fewer large, independent, long-term human facial studies than there are for established dermatology ingredients such as sunscreen filters, retinoids, niacinamide, or certain exfoliating acids. It is reasonable to view NAD+-related skincare as supportive rather than as a proven treatment for under-eye wrinkles or dark circles.

The phrase NAD face cream under eye wrinkles dark circles evidence dermatology nicotinamide riboside topical NAD skincare should therefore be interpreted cautiously: it points to a promising ingredient category, not a guaranteed fix. Be wary of claims that a topical cream can “reverse aging,” replace procedures, or treat medical causes of pigmentation or swelling.

How to judge an under-eye face cream

For the under-eye area, formulation matters as much as the headline ingredient. A cream should be comfortable, non-stinging, and supportive of the skin barrier. Dryness can exaggerate fine lines, so humectants and emollients often make the most visible short-term difference by smoothing the look of crepey texture.

  • Look for barrier-supportive ingredients such as glycerin, fatty alcohols, ceramides, squalane, or other well-tolerated moisturizers.
  • Avoid applying strong acids, high-strength retinoids, or fragranced products close to the lash line unless directed by a clinician.
  • Patch test first, especially if your eyelids are sensitive or reactive.
  • Use sunscreen and sunglasses during the day, because UV exposure worsens lines and discoloration over time.
  • Apply a small amount; more product near the eyes can increase irritation or migration into the eye.

If you already use prescription skincare, have eczema-prone eyelids, rosacea around the eyes, chronic allergies, or persistent swelling, check with a dermatologist before adding new actives. The eye area is a common place for irritant and allergic contact dermatitis.

Realistic expectations for wrinkles and dark circles

A NAD-focused face cream may help skin look more hydrated, smoother, and less tired if dryness is a major factor. It may support a healthier-looking skin barrier over time. However, it is unlikely to erase deeper expression lines, significantly lift hollow tear troughs, or correct dark circles driven mainly by anatomy or genetics.

For pigmentation-related darkness, improvement usually depends on daily sun protection and ingredients with better-established pigment data. For shadowing from facial structure, skincare can soften texture but cannot change underlying volume. For puffiness, sleep, salt intake, allergies, and fluid retention may matter more than any single cream.

If you want to try this category, use it consistently but conservatively: apply to clean, dry skin, keep it outside the immediate waterline, and stop if burning, redness, flaking, or swelling occurs.
