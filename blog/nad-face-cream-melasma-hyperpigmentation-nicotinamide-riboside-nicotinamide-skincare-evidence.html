NAD face cream melasma hyperpigmentation nicotinamide riboside nicotinamide skincare evidence: what it can and cannot mean

Melasma is a medical pigment condition influenced by ultraviolet light, visible light, hormones, heat, inflammation, and genetics. A cosmetic face cream should not be positioned as a melasma treatment. If you have new, worsening, patchy, or persistent discoloration, a dermatologist can confirm what it is and discuss evidence-based options.

NAD+ is involved in cellular energy metabolism and repair pathways, and interest in NAD-related skincare has grown for that reason. However, topical NAD+ skincare evidence is still early. There is not enough high-quality clinical evidence to say that an NAD face cream clears melasma or reverses hyperpigmentation. A more realistic role is skin support: hydration, barrier comfort, and compatibility with a broader routine.

Where nicotinamide riboside and nicotinamide fit

Nicotinamide riboside is a form of vitamin B3 studied mainly as an NAD+ precursor. In skincare, the key question is not just whether an ingredient is interesting in biology, but whether the finished topical formula has human skin data for the concern being discussed. For pigmentation, the evidence base is more established for certain other ingredients and prescription approaches than for topical NAD+ precursors.

Nicotinamide, also called niacinamide, has a stronger history in cosmetic skincare than nicotinamide riboside. It is commonly used to support the skin barrier, improve the look of dullness, reduce the appearance of redness from irritation, and help skin tolerate routines better. Some research suggests niacinamide can help the appearance of uneven tone, but melasma is complex and often needs dermatologist-guided care.

What to look for in a routine for melasma-prone uneven tone

The most important daily step for melasma-prone skin is consistent photoprotection. Broad-spectrum sunscreen, enough applied quantity, reapplication when outdoors, hats, shade, and minimizing heat exposure matter. Visible light can also be relevant for some people with melasma, so ask a dermatologist whether a tinted mineral sunscreen with iron oxides makes sense for your skin tone and lifestyle.

  • Look for barrier-supportive moisturizers that reduce dryness, tightness, and irritation.
  • Avoid harsh scrubs, aggressive at-home peels, and stacking too many actives at once.
  • Introduce new ingredients slowly, especially if you use retinoids, acids, or prescription pigment treatments.
  • Patch test if your skin is reactive or if pigmentation tends to worsen after irritation.
  • Check with a dermatologist before adding new actives if you have diagnosed melasma, are pregnant, are breastfeeding, or are using prescription treatments.

How to use an NAD face cream cautiously

If you choose an NAD-focused moisturizer, treat it as a supportive cosmetic step, not the main plan for melasma. Use it after cleansing and before sunscreen in the morning, or as a moisturizer at night. If you already use strong actives, introduce it on alternate days first so you can tell whether your skin likes the formula.

Pay attention to tolerance. Burning, persistent stinging, scaling, or worsening discoloration are signs to stop and reassess. Hyperpigmentation can be aggravated by inflammation, so a “more is better” approach is risky. Gentle consistency usually serves uneven tone better than frequent changes.
